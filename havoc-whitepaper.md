# HAVOC — High Altitude Venus Operational Concept
**Whitepaper: NASA Langley Study (Arney & Jones, 2015)**

## Origin

HAVOC was developed by Dale Arney and Chris Jones at NASA Langley's Space Mission Analysis Branch (SMAB). After a meeting on human Mars missions, they asked what a crewed Venus architecture would look like — and found Venus surprisingly more tractable than Mars at the right altitude.

It was an internal study to develop analysis skills and test tools, but it produced one of the most influential Venus human-exploration concepts ever published.

## Why Venus?

At **50 km altitude**:
- Pressure ≈ 1 bar (Earth-like)
- Temperature ≈ 70°C (survivable with cooling)
- Gravity ≈ 0.9 g
- Radiation protection comparable to Earth's surface (thick CO2 atmosphere)
- Solar flux ≈ 1.9× Earth orbit (2600 W/m²)

The surface, by contrast: 464°C, 92 bar. No lander has survived more than hours.

## Five Phases

| Phase | Mission | Duration | Vehicle |
|-------|---------|----------|--------|
| I | Robotic exploration | — | 31 m airship, 8 m tall |
| II | Crewed Venus orbit | 30 days | Orbital assembly, return module pre-deployed |
| III | Crewed atmosphere | 30 days | 129 m × 34 m airship, 2 astronauts |
| IV | Crewed atmosphere | 1 year | Same class, extended |
| V | Permanent presence | Indefinite | Floating cloud city / station |

## Phase III Airship (Crewed)

- Length: 129 m (423 ft) — twice a Boeing 747
- Height: 34 m (112 ft)
- Solar array: >1,000 m² on top surface
- Payload capacity: ~70,000 kg (nearly 60,000 kg = ascent vehicle)
- Power: 20 kWe allocated to habitat + cryogenic management
- Propulsion: propeller-driven
- Gondola: habitat + Venus Ascent Vehicle (winged two-stage rocket)
- Inflation gas: helium

## Entry, Descent & Inflation (EDI)

The airship enters folded inside an aeroshell at ~7,200 m/s (25,750 km/h ≈ 16,000 mph). Sequence:

1. Aerocapture at Venus
2. Aeroshell for heat dissipation during entry
3. Parachute deployment to slow the craft
4. Aeroshell jettisoned
5. Airship unfurls and inflates with helium during descent
6. Nominal float altitude: ~50 km

Key phrase from the study: *"landing would represent a significant failure of the mission"* — the concept is entry, descent, and inflation, never landing.

## Key Technical Challenges

1. **Aerocapture** at Venus and Earth (high-energy maneuvers)
2. **Rapid airship inflation** during descent
3. **Sulfuric acid protection** for solar panels and structure
4. Human-scale aeroentry vehicles
5. High-dynamic-pressure supersonic decelerators
6. Long-duration cryogenic storage (for the ascent vehicle's propellants)

## Proofs of Concept (2015)

### 1. Acid-Resistant Transparent Materials
FEP-Teflon (Fluorinated Ethylene Propylene) maintained **90% transmittance** to relevant solar spectra after 30 days of immersion in concentrated sulfuric acid. This validated solar-panel-protective films.

### 2. Airship Envelope Packaging Algorithm
A 1/53rd scale model demonstrated the airship envelope could be packaged to fit inside a Saturn V-class aeroshell and deploy correctly during EDI.

## Mission Timeline

- Outbound: 110 Earth days
- Stay: 30 days (Phase III) or 1 year (Phase IV)
- Return: 300 Earth days
- **Total: ~440 days** using near-term propulsion
- Abort option: return immediately after arrival
- Launch windows: every ~1.5 years (vs. 2 years for Mars)

## Comparison to Mars

| Factor | Venus (50 km) | Mars (surface) |
|--------|---------------|----------------|
| Pressure | 1 bar | <0.01 bar |
| Gravity | 0.9 g | 0.38 g |
| Temperature | ~70°C | −60°C avg |
| Atmosphere | Thick, CO2 | Thin, CO2 |
| Radiation | Shielded | Exposed |
| EVA needed | Minimal | Essential |
| Total mission time | ~440 days | ~2.5–3 years |

Arney and Jones argued Venus could come *before* Mars.

## Open Problems for Esslinger & Co.

- FEP-Teflon long-term degradation under 1.9× solar UV and thermal cycling (30–90°C)
- Permanent cloud-city structural concepts (Phase V)
- In-situ resource utilization: CO2 → oxygen/fuel, atmospheric water harvesting
- Ascent vehicle cryogenic boil-off management over 30–365 days
- Communication relay architecture (Venus–Earth, 6–28 min light time)
